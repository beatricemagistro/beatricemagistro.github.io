Beatrice Magistro

Assistant Professor of AI Governance
Northeastern University

I study the political consequences of technological change. My research examines how citizens and political elites understand AI's economic effects, and how these beliefs shape policy preferences and political behavior.

My work appears or is forthcoming in the American Journal of Political Science, Public Opinion Quarterly, Environmental Politics, Political Science Research and Methods, Political Behavior, West European Politics, the European Journal of Political Research, Foreign Affairs, the Lancet, and Nature Medicine, among others.

My book, Who Thinks Like an Economist?, is forthcoming at Cambridge University Press.

Technology & Politics

My research examines the political consequences of technological change, particularly artificial intelligence and automation. I study how citizens and political elites understand AI's economic effects, and how these beliefs shape policy preferences and political behavior.

  • Causal Beliefs and Political Backlash Against AI — POQ, 2026
  • Women's Skepticism toward AI and Risk — PNAS Nexus, 2026
  • Attitudes Toward AI and Globalization — AJPS, 2025
  • Automation Threat and Vote Choice in Europe — West European Politics, 2025
  • The Coming AI Backlash — Foreign Affairs, 2025
  • Automation Trade-offs and Policies Addressing Job Loss — PSRM, 2024
  • Gender Gap in Attitudes Toward Technological Change — Socio-Economic Review, 2024
  • Automation, Populist and Nationalist Rhetoric — Political Behavior, 2024
  • Causal Beliefs about AI among Politicians and the Public — Working paper coming soon

Future Projects:

  • SSHRC Project (2025–2028): As collaborator on a SSHRC-funded project (PI: Sophie Borwein), I am examining how political elites frame technological change through legislative speech analysis, elite surveys, and public responsiveness experiments.
  • Russell Sage Foundation (2026-2028): I am the principal investigator on a project to field four waves of surveys tracking Americans' causal beliefs about AI over 18 months—the first US longitudinal tracker of AI workplace attitudes.
  • Mental Models of Economic Growth: In March 2026, Sophie Borwein and I launched a new survey with YouGov on mental models of economic growth, including a conjoint experiment examining whether citizens can maintain support for AI-driven creative destruction when benefits come bundled with visible job losses. We plan to write a paper on growth mental models and democratic sustainability, and one on public tolerance for Schumpeterian creative destruction in the AI era, with plans to expand cross-nationally.

Populism & Political Economy

My work lies at the intersection of political economy and political behavior. I focus on how individuals' political and policy preferences change in response to socioeconomic shocks, and why voters sometimes support policies that undermine social welfare.

  • Who Thinks Like an Economist? — Cambridge University Press, Forthcoming
  • How Populism Harms Prosperity — Journal of Evolutionary Economics, 2025
  • Economic Crisis and Trust in the European Parliament — EJPR, 2016
  • Anti-Immigration Parties in Italy — South European Society and Politics, 2021
  • Party Cues, Policy Information, and Economic Literacy — Journal of Public Policy, 2022
  • Economic Literacy and Policy Preferences in Italy — Economics & Politics, 2021

Climate & Environment

I examine how to bridge ideological divides on climate policy, including the effects of economic framing on carbon tax support and public attitudes toward solar geoengineering.

  • Negative Externalities and Carbon Tax Support — Environmental Politics, 2024
  • Partisanship, Framing, and Solar Geoengineering — npj Climate Action, 2025
  • Political Ideology and Solar Geoengineering — PLOS Climate, 2025
  • Climate Change Free Riders — Scientific Reports, 2024

Sports & Society

I use my passion for soccer to study discrimination and human rights.

  • Racial Bias in Italian Football — Sociology, 2023 (covered by The Guardian)
  • Sportswashing and Online Fan Behavior — Social Science Quarterly, 2025

COVID-19

From April 2020 to June 2021 I worked as a research assistant on the UW COVID-19 State Policy Project, the nation-leading effort in collecting daily data on social distancing policies in response to COVID-19 in the US.

  • The Pandemic Policy U-Turn — Perspectives on Politics, 2021
  • Governor Partisanship and Mask Mandates — State Politics & Policy Quarterly, 2021
  • Multiple publications in The Lancet and Nature Medicine
